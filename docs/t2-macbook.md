# t2-macbook-omarchy

A MacBookPro16,1 (16" 2019, T2, BCM4364B3 Wi-Fi and Bluetooth) running Omarchy on the stable channel, beside macOS: the macOS volume stays on the disk because the Wi-Fi firmware comes from it.

## The T2 stack

Omarchy's stock kernel, `linux-omarchy`, with [KaiT2en](https://github.com/kaiT2en/KaiT2en-Fedora)'s T2 drivers built by DKMS, not `linux-t2`. They come from the signed `[t2-arch-packages]` repo (`github.com/josemiguelo/t2-arch-packages`, key `077D8295CCF834D3F5EC25F70506B72C34EB162F`, its README has the test results):

- `apple-t2-bridge-dkms` (KaiT2en's bridge stack; `t2bce-stack-dkms` until 2026-11-19): the T2's internal USB host (`apple_t2_vhci_hcd`, in the initramfs with `apple_t2_bridge` for the disk unlock prompt) that the keyboard, trackpad, Touch Bar, camera and Touch ID link hang off, the T2 audio device (`snd_apple_t2`) and the video encoders (`t2_ave`, `t2_sidecar`). The keyboard, Touch Bar and keyboard backlight run on KaiT2en's drivers on top of it (below).
- `t2-precision-trackpad-dkms`: the trackpad's multitouch and Force Touch. Stock kernels drive it as a plain mouse (no two-finger scroll, no tap to click; `linux-t2` patched `hid_magicmouse` for it). It loads first in the initramfs, so it holds the trackpad's interface before `hid-apple` can, which keeps the keyboard.
- `t2-touchbar-dkms` and `t2-touchbar`: the internal keyboard on `t2hid` (a `hid-apple` fork with the same options; in the initramfs for the unlock prompt, in `hid_apple`'s place in `apple-t2.conf`), the Touch Bar on `t2touchbar_kbd`/`_bl` and `t2bdrm` (its screen as a DRM device on a private seat), and the daemon drawing the bar (Apple's own row, with Esc, stays on the console and the unlock prompt; the bar switches to its screen when the graphical session starts and back when it ends, through `t2-touchbar-session@<UID>.service`): dark until touched or Fn, media and F-key layers (two-finger swipe on the lit bar, or hold Fn), two- and three-finger swipes on the dark bar for volume and brightness, haptics, the Touch ID prompt. The package installs the drivers inert (its `/usr/lib/modprobe.d/t2-touchbar.conf` blacklists them); the Omarchy fork's install switches over (`/etc/modprobe.d/t2-touchbar.conf` blacklisting `hid_apple`, `hid_appletb_*`, `appletbdrm`; `t2hid` in the initramfs; the `t2-touchbar` group; `systemctl --global enable t2-touchbar.service`), and `script.t2-key-swaps` adds the owner's layout (`/etc/modprobe.d/t2hid-swaps.conf`: Ctrl/Fn and Alt/Super swapped; at once and in the initramfs). The stock layout: delete `t2hid-swaps.conf`, `limine-mkinitcpio`.
- `t2-kbd-backlight`: the keyboard backlight fades out after 30 s without keyboard, trackpad or Touch Bar input and back on the next (enabled by the fork's install; timeout in `/etc/t2-kbd-backlight/kbd-backlight.toml`). Through UPower, so the Touch Bar's glyphs dim with it; the package's `t2-kbd-backlight-sync` tells UPower about the brightness keys too (Omarchy's write the LED behind its back). No ambient light sensor; Omarchy's own keyboard handling only turns it off when the screen locks.
- `t2-airshare` and `t2-drop`: Quick Share with Android phones (found over Bluetooth LE; the transfer over the shared Wi-Fi network, or over a hotspot the Mac opens beside its own connection, `t2qs0`, which drops the Wi-Fi link for 1-3 s as it comes and goes) and AirDrop with Apple devices (over AWDL, `awdl0`, from `t2-wireless-dkms`; the Mac stays on its Wi-Fi). `t2-drop` spots AirDrop senders through a passive BlueZ advertisement monitor (its package starts `bluetoothd -E` for it), not LE discovery, which kept paired LE mice and keyboards from reconnecting (below). The app's "visible" switch runs the receiver (`systemctl --user` `t2-airshare.service`) and makes the Mac discoverable over Bluetooth. The Omarchy fork's `fix-t2.sh` opens ufw for TCP 47901 (Quick Share), `t2qs0`, and TCP 8770 on `awdl0` (AirDrop), and for `t2-sidecar`'s Sunshine from private networks. Accept prompts are notifications with actions; "Always trust" matches devices by name only, so leave it off. In Omarchy: the T2 panel's AirShare tab (lit while visible; its panel has the visibility switch, send files, the clipboard or the last screenshot, the files received lately and the trusted devices; right-click on the T2 icon opens it while visible) and Share-menu entries beside LocalSend's (both shipped by the `t2-airshare` package and put in place by `t2-omarchy-setup`); sending picks the phone or Mac in the tab itself (the devices nearby, a PIN, progress), with no GTK window.
- `t2-wireless-dkms` (was `t2-bluetooth-dkms` until 2026-11-12): the kernel's `brcmfmac` (Wi-Fi) and `btbcm`, `hci_uart`, `hci_bcm4377` (Bluetooth) rebuilt from kernel.org 7.2.15 with KaiT2en's Apple Broadcom fixes 0001-0027 (pinned at `fd6e0cf`). Wi-Fi: resume through a firmware reload (each wake re-probes the card: ~4.7 s to reconnect against 3.6 s, and no `brcmf` errors against 6), TX beamforming and ARP/ND offload off, host-side AP handshake, AWDL (inert until something uses it). DKMS keeps the stock `brcmutil` and `brcmfmac-wcc/-cyw/-bca`: built from the patched tree they're the same code, so `dkms status` says "Differences between built and installed modules", harmlessly. Bluetooth: stock `btbcm` drops most of this Mac's LE advertisements (`journalctl -k | grep 'unknown advertising packet type'`), so LE devices (the Keychron K15 Max, the MX Master 3) never show up or can't connect. It only builds for 7.2.x `-omarchy` kernels: on 7.3 the stock Wi-Fi and Bluetooth drivers come back, with that bug, until the package moves to 7.3 sources.
- `t2smc-dkms` (replaces `applesmc`, which can't reach the T2 SMC on a stock kernel): fans, charge limit (`charge_control_end_threshold` on `BAT0`), sensors, the EC clock. `t2sbs-dkms` (replaces `sbs`): the battery percentage relative to full charge, not to design capacity.
- `t2smp-dkms`, `t2-thunderbolt-dkms` and the kernel arguments `pci=noaer pcie_ports=compat` (no `pm_async=off`): deep sleep wakes in about 1 s instead of 16. `pcie_ports=compat` costs native PCIe hotplug of Thunderbolt docks and eGPUs.
- `t2-fan-control` (replaces `t2fanrd`): its config is `/etc/t2-fancontrol/config.txt` with a system chill target of 80 °C. Upstream's 45 °C (the average of system and GPU sensors) pins this Mac's fans at full speed. The old `t2fanrd` curve throttled when it ramped late; watch for that under load with this curve.
- `t2-trackpad-actuator-dkms` and `t2-force-click`: the trackpad's Taptic Engine plays Apple's captured plain click on a force click (the second, harder press: a felt and audible second click, not macOS's deeper force-click haptic), and the app sets click pressure, the force click threshold and an action bound to it (its daemon, enabled by the fork's install, applies them at boot). `hid-apple` binds every interface of the trackpad/keyboard device, the haptic one included, so the package's udev rule moves that one (by its report descriptor) to the actuator, at boot (the coldplug `add`: the initramfs binds these, and doesn't carry the rule) and after each resume (`bind`).
- `t2-dsp` (with `bankstown-lv2`): KaiT2en's speaker and mic processing, the EQ, crossovers, compressor, limiters, crosstalk cancellation and virtual bass macOS applies to the 16" speakers, as PipeWire filter chains. A udev rule names the card `t2-16_1`; the default output is "Mac 16,1 native DSP Speakers" (the raw `HiFi__Speaker__sink` stays), the raw mic stays the default input (KaiT2en ranks its DSP mic lower). About 17% of one core while audio plays, nothing when idle; very low-latency apps (256-sample cycles) leave it little headroom. Pinned at KaiT2en's `6ddacd9`, on the bridge stack's audio driver `snd_apple_t2`.
- `t2-smc-control`: KaiT2en's `t2-smc-gaugingd` (its SMC Control window is the T2 panel's Battery tab). The battery stops at 85% (`args(script.t2-charge-limit, "90")`: the SMC's `CHLS`, which it keeps across reboots, stops 5 points below the limit; the script writes `/etc/t2-charge-limit.conf` for `t2-smc-control`'s `t2-charge-limit.service`, which sets it again at every boot since macOS may change it); a permanent limit keeps the gauge from recalibrating, so the daemon lifts it (`battery_charge_limit_suspended`) for a full charge when the gauge's DOD0 is 3 days old or the last full charge 21, as macOS does. The fork's install enables it. Its sleep hook `t2-low-battery` powers the Mac off after a wake on battery at 5 % or less (`T2_LOW_BATTERY_PERCENT` in `/etc/t2-low-battery.conf`), since it can't hibernate (below).
- `t2-dkms-guard`: aborts a `linux-omarchy` upgrade whose headers can't build these modules, so an update never drops the keyboard driver the disk unlock prompt needs. `sudo env T2_DKMS_GUARD_SKIP=1 pacman -Syu` overrides it.

Where it's built (`script.kait2en-dev` and `bare-repos` set all of it up, with the signing key from 1Password):

- **Packages**: `~/Repos/gh/josemiguelo/t2-arch-packages/all_worktrees/main`. `bin/kait2en-sync check` lists KaiT2en's commits per package since its pin (and the Fedora installer changes to review for `fix-t2.sh`), `apply` re-pins, builds, signs, tests and publishes, `kernel [channel]` builds every DKMS package against the newest `linux-omarchy-headers` of this Mac's channel or of the named one (`stable`, `rc`, `edge`). A daily user timer (`kait2en-sync.timer`, at idle CPU and disk priority) runs `notify`: a desktop notification for upstream updates, or for a new kernel the modules don't build against, on this Mac's channel or on rc, whose release candidates reach stable 1-2 weeks later.
- **Omarchy**: the fork `josemiguelo/omarchy`, whose only branch (and default) is `t2-kait2en-stable`, in `~/Repos/gh/josemiguelo/omarchy/all_worktrees/t2-kait2en-stable` (`install/hardware/apple/fix-t2.sh`, `install/hardware/pacman.sh`; `upstream` is `omacom/omarchy`). It's the T2 commits on the commit Omarchy's stable package is built from (the `_commit` in omarchy-pkgs' `pkgbuilds/omarchy/PKGBUILD`), rebased onto the next one at each release. A personal project, nothing goes upstream, so there's no `quattro`-based branch (until 2026-11-12, `t2-kait2en` was one; the fork's other branches, copies of omacom's, were deleted then, listed in `~/.cache/omarchy-fork-deleted-branches-2026-11-12.txt`).
- **ISO**: the `t2-kait2en` branch of the fork `josemiguelo/omarchy-iso`, in `~/Repos/gh/josemiguelo/omarchy-iso/all_worktrees/t2-kait2en`, built there with `bin/omarchy-iso-make --keep-pkg-cache --no-boot-offer --local-source ~/Repos/gh/josemiguelo/omarchy/all_worktrees/t2-kait2en-stable ~/Repos/gh/omacom/omarchy-pkgs --stable` (stable channel, `release/*-local-stable.iso`), and tested on fake T2 hardware with `VFIO_USER_SERVER=~/Repos/gh/nutanix/libvfio-user/build/samples/gpio-pci-idio-16 OMARCHY_PATH=~/Repos/gh/josemiguelo/omarchy/all_worktrees/t2-kait2en-stable bin/omarchy-iso-test <iso> --fake-pci 106b:1802:088000` (QEMU and libvfio-user's build deps are this machine's `omarchy_iso_test_deps`). In a VM, `t2-wifi-firmware` (no macOS volume) fails by design (with the old `t2bce` stack, until 2026-11-19, `systemd-modules-load` failed too: `t2bce_vhci` found no BCE on a fake device).

The ISO installs on stable. The first `omarchy update` replaces the branch's package (built as the recipe's `4.5.0-2`) with upstream's stable one. What `fix-t2.sh` set up stays: loadout guards the repo (`t2-arch-packages-repo`), and the `t2-kernel-args` package keeps the kernel arguments (its path unit and boot service write `t2-mac.conf` back when something rewrites it).

`loadouts/.etc/t2/` holds the rest: the packages aren't listed (the `t2-mac` meta-package carries them, and `t2-mac-hybrid` the hybrid graphics ones with `turbostat`; the fork's installer installs them, and `pacman -Syu` adds any new one), `t2-arch-packages-repo` keeps the repo, its key and a `pre-refresh-pacman` hook (`omarchy refresh pacman` rewrites `pacman.conf` without the repo); the `t2-*` scripts fix the hardware below.

## Things to know

- **Every `omarchy update` ends with a T2 check**: `t2PostUpdateCheck` keeps a hook in `~/.config/omarchy/hooks/post-update.d/` that runs `loadout status` for the T2 items and sends a critical notification naming any the update undid (a migration rewriting kernel arguments or initramfs modules, a pacman.conf refresh, the orphan cleanup); `loadout run <name>` repairs a script, `loadout install <name>` a package.

- **Its sshd lets in only named keys, from private networks**: `args(omarchySshd, …)` in `machines/profiles/t2-macbook.profile.loadout.ts` lists the GitHub key titles it trusts (`github-ssh-key` titles a machine's key with the machine's name), and the firewall opens 22 to the private IPv4 ranges only. A new machine that should ssh in needs its name added there; any other key gets no shell, by design.
- **Its Wi-Fi firmware comes from the macOS volume**: `apple-bcm-firmware-fetcher` mounts it read-only with the APFS driver (`linux-apfs-rw-dkms`: `linux-t2` built APFS in, `linux-omarchy` doesn't) and copies the firmware to `/lib/firmware/brcm`. Its post-install run during an ISO install happens in the installer's chroot, which can't load the installed kernel's driver, so `fix-t2.sh`'s `t2-wifi-firmware.service` fetches at the installed system's boots until the fetcher has recorded its files, before networking starts. So whenever a request touches reinstalling Omarchy here or macOS's partition, warn the user first: erasing or reformatting the macOS volume leaves the next install without Wi-Fi. The fallback is the old `apple-bcm-firmware` package (14.0-1, gone from the repos, conflicting with the fetcher), a 1Password document (Personal vault, titled `apple-bcm-firmware-14.0-1-any.pkg.tar.zst`; Apple's firmware can't go into this public repo): `script.t2-bcm-firmware` uses the fetcher when a macOS volume is there and the package otherwise, restoring it from a verified copy in `/var/lib/t2-bcm-firmware/` or from 1Password over USB tethering or USB Ethernet. Without either, Wi-Fi is dead until the firmware comes from t2linux's `firmware.sh` (method 5, a macOS Recovery image downloaded from Apple). From Omarchy 4.5, `omarchy-update` also removes orphaned packages without asking (`omarchy-update-orphan-pkgs -y`, every `pacman -Qtdq`), so whichever package provides the firmware must stay explicitly installed: never let it become a dependency (`pacman -D --asdeps`).
- **Its Bluetooth degrades over many sleeps**: the BCM4364's Bluetooth runs with no firmware patch (`brcm/BCM.hcd` not found; nothing ships one for this chip, and it's the same on `linux-omarchy`). After days of suspends, Bluetooth audio stutters, drops, or stays connected but silent. Restarting bluetooth or rebooting doesn't fix it; a full shutdown does. `t2-wireless-dkms` carries KaiT2en's ACL priority and LE fixes with its Wi-Fi ones.
- **Paired Bluetooth LE devices reconnect only while no LE discovery runs**: the kernel's reconnect scan gives way to discovery, so the MX Master 3 took ~26 s to reconnect after waking, or never, while `t2-drop` kept discovering for AirDrop senders (8 ms once it stopped, in `btmon`). Since 2026-11-19 `t2-drop` watches passively instead. Omarchy's Bluetooth panel also discovers while it's open, so a device reconnects slowly until it's closed. The dropped-advert bug (the owner's t2linux/linux-t2-patches#57, KaiT2en's 0012) was already fixed: `journalctl -k | grep 'unknown advertising packet type'` stays empty.
- **It can't hibernate**: the firmware's ACPI S4 step hangs, and in `shutdown` mode the Radeon, Wi-Fi and T2 bridge drivers don't survive the freeze and restore (tested 2026-11-19; neither KaiT2en stack has hibernation callbacks). Hibernation is removed here (`omarchy-hibernation-remove`), and the fork's `omarchy-hibernation-setup` skips T2 Macs. Instead `t2-low-battery` powers off after a low-battery wake: on 2026-11-17 the battery ran flat in sleep, which resets the clock and needs a macOS login before Touch ID works again.
- **Its clock lives in the EC**: `t2smc` reads and writes it (rtc1 on `linux-omarchy`), the legacy CMOS rtc (rtc0) only reads it. `t2smc` writes the system time back to it itself, in its suspend and shutdown callbacks (the kernel's NTP writeback stops at rtc0 and never reaches it); `script.t2-boot-clock`'s shutdown unit did the same again and was retired on 2026-11-12 and taken off every machine.
- **Its T2 has a network link of its own**: `enp4s0f1u1` ("Apple T2 Controller", `05ac:8233`, MAC `ac:de:48:00:11:22`) is macOS's line to the T2 chip, a CDC NCM function on the BCE bus. No DHCP server answers on it, so `t2-touchid`'s udev rule keeps NetworkManager off it. NetworkManager reads that tag only when a device appears, so a new rule takes effect at the next boot, or at once with `nmcli device set <iface> managed no`. Touch ID uses it: `t2-touchid` (t2-arch-packages) reaches the Secure Enclave over it, and its udev rule brings the link up as the device appears, since the T2 signals carrier only then (a link opened later stays NO-CARRIER until a `cdc_ncm` rebind or a reboot). `program.t2-touchid` and `script.t2-touch-id-auth` set it up: the daemon running (it tells Omarchy's fingerprint detection the reader is ready, through the package's `/usr/share/omarchy-platform/fingerprint-readers`), then Omarchy's own fingerprint setup, whose enroll step binds the macOS finger with one touch and which adds the PAM lines for sudo, polkit and the lock screen; Omarchy's menu removes it again; fingers are enrolled in macOS, which has to have been logged into once since the T2 last lost power.

## Reinstalling

Keeps this hostname, so the machine and its state file stay `t2-macbook-omarchy`.

1. macOS: restore it if it's gone, and leave free space for Omarchy (Disk Utility, shrink the APFS container). The macOS volume must survive: it holds the Wi-Fi firmware.
2. Omarchy: check the stick holds a stable build before booting it: `grep -E '^omarchy(-settings)?(-dev)? ' /run/media/$USER/OMARCHY_*/arch/pkglist.x86_64.txt` lists `omarchy-settings`, not `omarchy-settings-dev` (a `-dev` there is an ISO built without `--stable`, which installs edge). Then boot it (Option at power-on; the live system boots `linux-t2` for the keyboard) and install **into the free space**, never over the whole disk.
3. First boot: `t2-wifi-firmware.service` fetches the Wi-Fi firmware before networking starts and brings the card up on it (`/usr/local/sbin/t2-wifi-reload`); `systemctl status t2-wifi-firmware` says how it went. Then `omarchy update`, and the bootstrap in the README with this machine's name; `script.t2-omarchy-channel` switches an install that came up on another channel back to stable.

What the 2026-11-10 reinstall showed (an ISO built before the fixes below):

- **The install stopped in "Configuring system"**: `fix-fkeys.sh` ran `omarchy-keyboard-toprow`, whose live write to `/sys/module/hid_apple/parameters/fnmode` fails in the installer's chroot (`/sys` is read-only there) and, under `set -e`, ended the phase. The fake-T2 VM test never loads `hid_apple`, so only the real Mac hits it. The fork (`t2-kait2en-stable`) leaves T2 Macs out of `fix-fkeys.sh` altogether, so the write never runs on them. (The deleted `t2-kait2en` also skipped the write wherever `/sys` isn't writable, a fix for other Macs not carried over.) An install stopped this way can be finished from the live ISO: open the LUKS root, mount `@`, `@home`, `@log`, `@pkg` and `/boot`, `arch-chroot` in and run the rest of the configuration by hand. The installer's last phase, the read-only `@factory` snapshot that `omarchy-system-factory-reset` restores, is then skipped unless taken by hand.
- **`omarchy-toprow.conf`**: an install from an ISO before that fix leaves `/etc/modprobe.d/omarchy-toprow.conf` with `options hid_apple fnmode=1`. On `t2hid` it no longer applies (its options are `t2hid`'s); its other lines are for ASUS drivers, so it can go.
- **`t2-wifi-firmware.service` reported a failure** although Wi-Fi came up after a reboot: its `modprobe -r brcmfmac` ran into `brcmfmac_wcc`, which holds it.

What the 2026-11-11 reinstall showed:

- **It came up on edge**: the stick held an ISO built that morning, before omarchy-iso's `--stable` existed, so it installed `omarchy-dev` and the edge repo. Nothing said so until `pacman -Q`; `script.t2-omarchy-channel` now catches it, and step 2 checks the stick.
- **No Wi-Fi on the first boot, fine after a reboot**: `brcmfmac` probes at coldplug, before `t2-wifi-firmware` has copied the firmware, and fails (`brcmfmac4364b3-pcie.bin failed with error -2`). Its pending firmware request holds the module for ~4 s more, then unbinds the card (with a `cancel_work_sync` warning in `brcmf_pcie_remove`). The fetcher's own reload runs inside that window, its `modprobe -r brcmfmac` fails, and it ignores the error. The fork's unit now runs `t2-wifi-reload` after the fetcher: it retries removing the driver (and its holders) until the request lets go, loads it again, and fails the unit only if the card still has no interface. `fix-t2.sh` can't name the driver (`test/shell.d/brcmfmac-supplicant-test.sh` keeps `brcmfmac` to one leaf), so the script finds it from the card's PCI class and modalias.
